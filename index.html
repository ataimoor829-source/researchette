The idea

Most students are told to “write a synopsis”. Nobody shows them how.

Supervisors are busy, and YouTube videos can’t read your draft. So students copy old theses, guess at the format and lose months going back and forth.

I started Researchette to fix that. I have been guiding students through their research for years, and the method is always the same: break the work into small pieces, explain each piece properly, let the student try, then correct it together. Researchette turns that method into a daily routine you can follow from anywhere.

You won’t get a paper written for you. You will learn to write it yourself, with someone checking every step.

The roadmap

Ten steps, in the order research is actually written.

You unlock the next step once a mentor approves the current one, so you never build on a weak foundation.

  1. Research title

    Variables, population, place. Narrow a vague topic into a researchable one.

  2. Problem statement

    What is wrong, who it affects, and why it hasn’t been solved yet.

  3. Objectives & questions

    SMART objectives and research questions that match them one to one.

  4. Introduction

    Background, significance and the gap your study fills.

  5. Literature review

    Finding, reading and synthesising sources instead of summarising them.

  6. Methodology

    Design, sampling, instruments and ethics, justified for your study.

  7. Data & analysis

    Collecting data and choosing the right test or coding approach.

  8. Results & discussion

    Reporting findings clearly and linking them back to the literature.

  9. Referencing

    APA 7th edition, in-text citations and avoiding plagiarism.

  10. Synopsis or thesis

    Putting it all together into one polished, submittable document.

How to write a research title

A formula and a 5-point checklist.

[Effect / Relationship / Role] of [Variable 1] on [Variable 2] among [Population] in [Place]

  1. Name your main variables.
  2. Say who you are studying.
  3. Say where, if it matters.
  4. Keep it under about 15–20 words.
  5. Remove filler like “A study on…”.

APA 7 referencing basics

The formats you will use most.

Journal article: Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article. Journal Name, Volume(Issue), pages. https://doi.org/xxxx

In-text: (Khan & Ali, 2023) or Khan and Ali (2023). Three or more authors: (Khan et al., 2023).

Synopsis structure

The sections most universities expect.
  1. Title
  2. Introduction and background
  3. Problem statement
  4. Objectives and research questions / hypotheses
  5. Significance of the study
  6. Brief literature review
  7. Methodology
  8. Timeline (work plan)
  9. References

Always check your own department’s format first.

ToolOpen

Literature review matrix

Stop summarising. Start comparing.

Make a table with one row per source and these columns:

  • Author & year
  • Aim
  • Sample & method
  • Key findings
  • Limitations
  • How it relates to your study

Patterns across rows become the paragraphs of your review.

GuideOpen

Searching Google Scholar well

Find better sources faster.
  • Put exact phrases in quotes: "sleep quality"
  • Use OR for synonyms: Instagram OR "social media"
  • Filter to the last 5–7 years using “Since”.
  • Open “Cited by” on a strong paper to find newer work.
ChecklistOpen

6 common methodology mistakes

What supervisors send back most often.
  1. No justification for the research design.
  2. Sample size with no calculation or reference.
  3. Sampling technique doesn’t match the population.
  4. Instrument source and reliability not reported.
  5. Ethics and consent left out.
  6. Analysis plan doesn’t answer the research questions.
