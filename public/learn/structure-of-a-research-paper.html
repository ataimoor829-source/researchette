Structure of a research paper (IMRaD) and word count

Home › Learn research › The shape of a paper

Almost every research paper uses the same order, called IMRaD: Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion. The title and abstract sit on top; the conclusion and references come at the end. Once you know the order, you always know what goes where.

For the main text, from Introduction to Conclusion. The abstract, tables and references are counted separately. Always check your target journal’s own limit.

The parts, in order

  1. Title and abstract

    The shop window. Most people read only these, so they must tell the whole story in a few lines.

  2. Introduction: why?

    What is known, what is missing, and what you set out to find.

  3. Methods: how?

    Exactly what you did, so someone else could repeat it.

  4. Results: what did you find?

    Only the numbers and facts. No opinions yet.

  5. Discussion: what does it mean?

    Explain the results, compare with other studies, admit the limits.

  6. Conclusion and references

    The take-home message in 2 or 3 lines, then the list of papers you used.

Tips

  • Write Methods first: it is the easiest, because you already know what you did.
  • Then Results, then Discussion, then Introduction.
  • Write the Abstract and Title last, once everything else is done.

The order, with word counts

Title (12–15 words)
Abstract (about 250 words: Objective · Methods · Results · Conclusion) + 3–6 keywords
1. Introduction (350–450 words)
2. Objectives (1–2 sentences)
3. Methods (500–700 words)
4. Results (400–600 words + 2–4 tables)
5. Discussion (700–1000 words, ending with Limitations)
6. Conclusion (50–80 words)
References (15–30)

Main text total: about 2500–3000 words.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Writing the abstract first, then having to rewrite it.
  • Putting results inside the Methods, or opinions inside the Results.
  • Mixing the order because a friend’s paper looked different. Check your target journal’s instructions.
